Skill Profile · Grade 3 Math

Performance-level descriptors

Each level's score range is set by your percentage bands. These descriptors are a starting template — tap any one to tailor the wording to your goal, so it becomes the shared language you'll use to place and grow students.

Well Above Typical
Student independently and consistently demonstrates this skill with a high level of accuracy and efficiency, flexibly applying strategies to complex or unfamiliar tasks and communicating their reasoning with precise academic vocabulary and detailed explanations.
Above Typical
Student accurately demonstrates this skill using appropriate strategies, explains their reasoning clearly, shows strong conceptual understanding, and applies it independently in most grade-level situations.
Typical
Student demonstrates this skill on grade-level tasks with occasional teacher support and can explain their thinking, though accuracy or consistency may vary.
Below Typical
Student demonstrates partial understanding and requires frequent support; may struggle to select effective strategies, use models, or clearly explain their reasoning.
Well Below Typical
Student demonstrates limited understanding and cannot yet complete grade-level tasks consistently, even with significant support, and has difficulty explaining their thinking.
These cut points and descriptors are the shared scale for this SLO — the same one that colour-codes your BOY, MOY and EOY data. You'll finalize and lock everything after you build your Personal Plan — so you can still adjust the wording and cut points until then.

Which rubric is your district using this year?

Not sure? Choose T-TESS — it's what almost every district uses in 2026–27. You can change this any time.

What's coming with T-TESS 2?

T-TESS 2 still has four domains, but two are renamed with shifted scope — Planning becomes Instructional Preparation and Instruction becomes Instructional Delivery; Learning Environment and Professional Practices keep their names. The 16 dimensions reorganise into 11, each framed as a guiding question, and some move between domains (Data & Assessment shifts to Delivery; Differentiation shifts to Preparation). 2026–27 is a 15-system pilot; 2027–28 is TEA's recommended adoption year; 2028–29 is the fallback. The higher levels attach explicit percentages to student evidence, and Lesson Design applies only to teachers not using SBOE-approved HQIM.

Your evidence carries forward, but not automatically — Communication (2.3) and Managing Student Behavior (3.2) have no direct successor and get distributed elsewhere. We'll walk you through re-tagging rather than pretend it's seamless.

Sources: TEA T-TESS Refresh; T-TESS 2 Pilot Rubric. TEA's letter says 12 dimensions; the published rubric contains 11 — we follow the rubric.
