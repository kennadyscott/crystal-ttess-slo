## 1. What actually changed — and it is much more than "16 → 12"

| | Current T-TESS | T-TESS 2 Pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Domains | 4 (Planning, Instruction, Learning Environment, Professional Practices) | 4, **renamed** (Instructional Preparation, Instructional **Delivery**, Learning Environment, Professional Practices & Responsibilities) |
| Dimensions | 16, numbered `1.1`–`4.4` | **11**, lettered `D1.A`–`D4.C`, each phrased as a **guiding question** |
| Sub-structure | 274 free-standing indicator bullets, restated in full at each level | **37 named "target skills"**, each with 5 levels — **185 cells** |
| Level naming | Distinguished → Improvement Needed | **Level 5 Distinguished → Level 1 Improvement Needed** (numbered) |
| Level logic | Each level restates everything with softer qualifiers | **Cumulative "AND" layering** — L4 = L3 + one new skill; L5 = L4 + one more |
| Evidence bar | Vague qualifiers ("all", "most", "few") | **Explicit percentages** plus a global **80% rule** |
| Conditionality | None | **D1.A applies only to teachers NOT using SBOE-approved HQIM** |

> **⚠️ Discrepancy to resolve.** TEA's *T-TESS Refresh: Engagement Update and Pilot* letter states T-TESS 2 has **12 dimensions**. The published pilot rubric contains **11** (D1.A/B/C, D2.A/B/C, D3.A/B, D4.A/B/C). Either the letter counts something the PDF doesn't, or the letter predates a revision. **Do not quote "12" in the product** until this is resolved with TEA — the app currently says 12 and should be softened to "around a dozen" or corrected. This is the first thing to fix.

## 2. The five findings that matter

### 2.1 The rubric went quantitative — and that turns CI from evidence into instrument
This is the most consequential change in the document, and it is unambiguously good for Crystal Instruction.

T-TESS 1 graded student evidence with words an appraiser judged by impression: *all* / *most* / *some* / *few*. **T-TESS 2 attaches numbers.**

- **D2.C.1 Student Progress** — the percentage *is* the descriptor: `<20%` (L1) · `21–60%` (L2) · **`61–80%` (L3 Proficient)** · `81–95%` (L4) · `96–100%` (L5).
- **D2.B.4 Student Practice and Cognitive Work** — L4 requires "**Most** students… **(81–95%)**", L5 "**Almost all or all** students… **(96% to 100%)**".
- **Global rule (p1):** *"for a specific performance level to be considered met, the descriptor must be met by the teacher or student **at least 80% of the time** during the observation or summative period."*

A teacher cannot produce those figures from memory or from a walkthrough impression. **CI produces them natively** — per-student exit-ticket mastery, per-item correctness in Live Monitor, Crystal Reports per-standard percentages against teacher-set bands. Under T-TESS 1, CI was *supporting evidence*. Under T-TESS 2, **CI is the measurement instrument the rubric now presupposes.**

*Product consequence:* Crystal Reports should express results in the rubric's own bands. A "D2.C.1: 78% — Level 3" readout would be the single most useful thing the platform could show a Texas teacher in 2027–28. That is a small build on data CI already has.

### 2.2 The HQIM exemption is a strategic fork, not a detail
D1.A — the whole **Lesson Design** dimension, 5 of the rubric's 37 indicators — is prefaced: **"ONLY APPLICABLE FOR TEACHERS NOT USING SBOE-APPROVED HQIM MATERIALS."** D1.B and D1.C are marked "APPLICABLE FOR ALL TEACHERS."

So a teacher on SBOE-approved HQIM is rated on **10 dimensions / 32 indicators**; a teacher not on HQIM is rated on **11 / 37**.

This matters to ClearK12 three ways, and the answer is a positioning decision rather than a product one:

1. **Bluebonnet Learning is Texas's SBOE-approved HQIM and it is already a Curriculum Connect provider** (Bluebonnet Math and Bluebonnet ELAR both appear in the live product). So a meaningful share of CI's own customers will skip D1.A.
2. **If CI content is positioned as the primary curriculum**, its teachers land in the non-HQIM branch and get judged on D1.A.5 *Curriculum Evaluation and Selection*, which asks whether the teacher "evaluates, selects, and adapts **district-provided** materials… to align with grade-level rigor" and at L5 "can explain how each resource used supports coherence, rigor, and long-term learning goals **across units**." That is a harder row for a teacher leaning on a third-party library.
3. **If CI is positioned as a supplement layered on HQIM**, D1.A disappears for those teachers and CI's value concentrates in D1.B, D1.C and Domain 2 — where it is genuinely strong.

**Recommendation:** position CI explicitly as an HQIM-complement, and pursue SBOE approval or an HQIM partnership if the ambition is to be the core. The rubric now rewards the complement posture.

### 2.3 ELPS is named in the rubric, and CI cannot record it
Under T-TESS 1 the missing student-profile data was a soft weakness inside 1.3. **T-TESS 2 names it at Proficient in two places:**

- **D1.A.1** is literally titled *"TEKS **and ELPS** Aligned Lessons"* — L3 requires "lesson objectives aligned to the TEKS **and ELPS**."
- **D1.C.1 Planned Differentiation** L3 requires differentiation meeting "the diverse needs of learners, **including students with disabilities, gifted and talented students, and emergent bilingual students**."

CI's student record holds name, ID, grade, credentials and class — **no ELPS proficiency level, no emergent-bilingual designation, no IEP/504, no GT flag.** Every asset is tagged TEKS; **none is tagged ELPS.**

And the timing compounds: **ELPS moves from 4 to 5 proficiency levels in 2026–27** — the pilot year. So the rubric starts naming ELPS in the same year the ELPS framework itself changes, and CI can record neither the old levels nor the new.

*This promotes the student-profile gap from "nice to have" to the highest-priority data-model change in the platform.* An ELPS tag on assets plus EB/SpEd/GT flags on students is now a rubric requirement, not a differentiation nicety.

### 2.4 The student-centred ceiling survives — but is now precisely locatable
Page 1 states the design: *"Many performance levels progress from examining the actions of teachers to the actions of students,"* with a two-way arrow between **Teacher Actions** and **Student Actions**. Same ceiling as T-TESS 1.

The improvement is that the AND-layering shows **exactly which level flips.** Three representative ladders:

| Indicator | L3 Proficient | L4 Accomplished | L5 Distinguished |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D2.C.2** Checks for Understanding | *Teacher* deliberately checks — **CI PROVES** | *Teacher* uses the info to **provide feedback** — **CI NO HELP** (no feedback field) | ***Students*** use feedback to refine understanding — **CI NO HELP** |
| **D2.C.3** Monitoring and Responding | *Teacher* monitors and analyses — **CI PROVES** | *Teacher* makes **adjustments** — **CI NO HELP** (no action log) | ***Students*** monitor own learning — **CI NO HELP** |
| **D2.A.1** Alignment to Objective | *Teacher* provides aligned instruction — **CI PROVES** | ***Students*** can explain the lesson and objectives — **CI NO HELP** | ***Students*** connect to previous learning and other content areas — **CI NO HELP** |

The pattern is consistent: **CI reliably delivers L3 across Domain 2 and is blocked at L4 by exactly the two features already identified as gaps #1 and #3 in the T-TESS 1 analysis** — the action/adjustment log and student feedback. T-TESS 2 makes those two gaps the precise, citable difference between Proficient and Accomplished in the platform's home domain. That is a much stronger roadmap argument than T-TESS 1 gave us.

### 2.5 Two new places CI wins that had no T-TESS 1 equivalent

**D1.B Instructional Internalization** is a wholly new dimension, and CI is unusually well-shaped for it. L3 requires the teacher prepare "by reviewing and pacing lesson components, completing tasks and assessments, and selecting appropriate embedded supports, through consistent use of applicable **district-approved tools for internalization**." The ClearLesson **Preview** mode (filmstrip, slide-by-slide, Student Device View), the **About** panel (Focuses On / Builds On / Next Level / Read More) and per-slide **Teacher Tips** are literally internalization tooling. If ClearK12 gets CI named as a district-approved internalization tool, D1.B.1 becomes a PROVES.

**D4.A.3 Accurate Records** has no T-TESS 1 counterpart — old 4.1 was purely ethics and attendance. L3 is "maintains thorough and accurate records with occasional prompting"; L4 "independently"; **L5 "AND teacher supports students by applying knowledge of their progress and learning plans through records."** CI is a record-keeping system whose entire purpose is applying progress knowledge. This is a free win nobody is claiming.

| 2.4 Differentiation | D1.C + D2.A.4 | **Moved domains** (Instruction → Preparation) |
| 2.5 Monitor & Adjust | D2.C + D1.B.3 | Mostly clean |
| 3.1 Environment & Routines | D3.A.2/.3 | Clean |
| 3.2 Managing Behavior | D3.A.1 | **Absorbed** — no longer its own dimension |
| 3.3 Classroom Culture | D3.B | Renamed *Student Persistence* |
| 4.1 Demeanor & Ethics | D4.A | Expanded (technology, records) |
| 4.2 Goal Setting | D4.B.1 | Clean |
| 4.3 Professional Development | D4.B.2/.3 | Clean |
| 4.4 School Community | D4.C | **Promoted** to full dimension |

**Three problems with the current claim:**

1. **Two dimensions dissolve.** *2.3 Communication* and *3.2 Managing Student Behavior* have no T-TESS 2 successor — their indicators scatter. Evidence tagged `2.3` cannot be auto-migrated to a single target.
2. **Four mappings cross domains.** 1.2 moves Planning → Delivery; 2.4 moves Instruction → Preparation. So "the domains don't change, therefore evidence maps forward" — the reasoning currently in the app — **is not sound.** The domains persist but dimensions move between them.
3. **The bar rose.** Evidence sufficient for T-TESS 1 Proficient may not clear T-TESS 2 Level 3. Concretely: 1.1-aligned evidence no longer suffices for D1.A.1 without **ELPS** alignment; 1.3 evidence no longer suffices for D1.C.1 without **named-population** differentiation; 1.2/2.5 evidence no longer reaches L4 without **feedback**. Evidence carried forward can *land at a lower level*.

**Suggested product wording:** *"T-TESS 2 keeps the four domains and reorganises 16 dimensions into 11. Your evidence stays yours and most of it re-tags cleanly — but two dimensions (Communication, Managing Student Behavior) are dissolved into others, some move between domains, and several indicators raise the bar. When your district adopts, we'll walk you through re-tagging rather than pretend it's automatic."*

Also: **correct or soften the "12 dimensions" figure** currently in the app (see §1).

### Net assessment
**T-TESS 2 is better for Crystal Instruction than T-TESS 1, on balance.** The quantification of student-evidence descriptors (§2.1) plays directly to the platform's only unique asset — per-student, per-item, per-standard data — and two new indicators (D1.B.1 internalization, D4.A.3 records) are near-free wins. CI can credibly reach **Level 5** on two indicators under T-TESS 2 versus one contestable Distinguished bullet under T-TESS 1.

But the rubric also names three requirements CI structurally cannot meet today — **ELPS tagging, named-population differentiation data, and year-long scope-and-sequence coverage** — and it promotes **family partnership** to a full dimension where CI is at zero. The product gap list is unchanged in substance but sharper in citation, and the priority order shifts:
